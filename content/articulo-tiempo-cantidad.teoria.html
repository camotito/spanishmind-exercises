The article with time, days and prices

What time is it? Es la una, son las dos

The question is ¿Qué hora es? / What time is it?

La hora = the hour (think horology). Spanish says "it is the hour two": the word hora is understood, and it is feminine, so the article is la or las.

  • Es la una. / It's one o'clock. (one hour: singular)
  • Son las dos. / It's two o'clock. (more than one: plural)
  • Son las ocho. / It's eight o'clock.

Only la una is singular. Every other hour is las.

Minutes: y and menos

Y = and. Menos = minus. The minutes are added to the hour or taken away from it.

  • Son las tres y veinte. / It's three and twenty (3:20).
  • Es la una y veinticinco. / It's one and twenty-five (1:25).
  • Son las cuatro menos diez. / It's four minus ten (3:50).

Two times have their own word:

  • y cuarto = and a quarter (think quart): Son las once y cuarto. (11:15)
  • y media = and half: Son las ocho y media. (8:30)

After half past, Spanish speakers usually count back to the next hour with menos: Es la una menos cuarto. (12:45)

At what time? A la una, a las tres

¿A qué hora? / At what time? The answer uses a (at) and keeps la / las.

  • La fiesta es a las nueve. / The party is at nine.
  • El partido es a las diez. / The match is at ten.
  • Nos vemos a la una. / We'll see each other at one.

Compare: Son las tres = It is three. A las tres = At three.

✗ en las tres, ✗ a tres. ✓ a las tres.

Morning, afternoon, night

Spanish speakers talk in 12 hours and add the part of the day.

  • las siete de la mañana / 7 in the morning
  • las tres de la tarde / 3 in the afternoon
  • las diez de la noche / 10 at night

To say in the morning as a habit, use por: por la mañana, por la tarde, por la noche. They always have la.

  • Normalmente estudiamos por la noche. / We usually study at night.
  • Trabajo por la tarde. / I work in the afternoon.

Days: el lunes, los lunes

Spanish has no word for on with days. It uses the article.

  • el = one particular day: Tenemos un examen el lunes. / We have an exam on Monday.
  • los = every week: Diana sale los sábados. / Diana goes out on Saturdays.

Monday to Friday do not change in the plural, only the article does: el martes, los martes. El martes tenemos clase and Los martes tenemos clase are both correct.

Saturday and Sunday add -s: el sábado, los sábados; el domingo, los domingos.

✗ en lunes. ✓ el lunes.

Dates: el doce de febrero

The day of the month takes el. The month does not take an article.

  • Mi cumpleaños es el doce de febrero. / My birthday is on the 12th of February.
  • Nací el dos de diciembre. / I was born on the 2nd of December.
  • Tengo vacaciones en agosto. / I have a holiday in August.

With a day and a date together, the article goes before the day only: Felipe se casa el martes 13 de julio.

No article: hoy es, mañana es

When you say what day today is, there is no article.

  • Hoy es jueves, cuatro de octubre. / Today is Thursday, 4th October.
  • Mañana es sábado. / Tomorrow is Saturday.

Compare: Hoy es jueves (what day it is) and La reunión es el jueves (when something happens).

How often: al día, a la semana

A + the article means per. Literally, dos veces a la semana = twice at the week.

  • Duermo ocho horas al día. / I sleep eight hours a day.
  • Voy al gimnasio dos días a la semana. / I go to the gym two days a week.
  • Vamos de vacaciones una vez al año. / We go on holiday once a year.

A + el always becomes al: al día, al mes, al año. With la nothing changes: a la semana.

Prices: el kilo, la docena

For per with prices and measures, Spanish uses just the article, with no a.

  • Dos euros el kilo. / Two euros a kilo. (lit. two euros the kilo)
  • Cuatro pesos el litro. / Four pesos a litre.
  • Dos euros la docena. / Two euros a dozen.
  • Un peso los cien gramos. / One peso per 100 grams.

The article agrees with the word that follows: el kilo, el litro, la docena, los cien gramos.

Heads-up

Written in numbers, an hour is usually 7:30 or 19:30, but when speaking, 12 hours with de la tarde is much more common than las diecinueve.

Timetables, trains and TV schedules are the exception: there, the 24-hour clock is normal.
