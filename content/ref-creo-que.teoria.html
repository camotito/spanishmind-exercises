creo que, sé que

que

que = that.

It joins two sentences. Each one has its own verb.

In English you can leave out that. In Spanish que always stays.

  • creo que = I think (that)
  • pienso que = I think (that) (think pensive)
  • sé que = I know (that)

Examples:

  • Creo que mañana va a llover. = I think it's going to rain tomorrow.
  • Pienso que Roma es cara. = I think Rome is expensive.
  • Sé que él nunca trabaja los sábados. = I know he never works on Saturdays.

Typical mistakes:

  • Creo mañana va a llover. → incorrect
  • Creo que mañana va a llover. → correct

supongo que sí

  • supongo = I suppose
  • Supongo que sí. = I suppose so. (literally: I suppose that yes)
  • Supongo que no. = I suppose not. (literally: I suppose that no)
  • Creo que sí. = I think so.
  • Creo que no. = I don't think so.

Examples:

  • Supongo que va a hacer calor. = I suppose it's going to be hot.

Two que in one sentence

In ¿Crees que tienes que trabajar? the first que means that, and the second one belongs to tener que.

  • ¿Crees que tienes que trabajar el sábado? = Do you think you have to work on Saturday?
