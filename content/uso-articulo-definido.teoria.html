Definite article: when to use it

Abstract nouns and general concepts

When speaking about a general concept, an entire category, or an abstract idea.

  • El amor es ciego. / Love is blind.
  • La tecnología cambia vidas. / Technology changes lives.
  • Los perros son fieles. / Dogs are loyal. (The entire species, not specific dogs.)
  • Hablamos de la guerra. / We talk about war.

Days of the week and time

Days of the week are masculine and require an article. They do not take a preposition, whereas English requires one (on). Telling the time always requires a feminine article.

  • Te veo el lunes. / I'll see you on Monday.
  • Los sábados no trabajo. / I don't work on Saturdays.
  • Son las tres. / It's three o'clock.

Body parts and personal belongings

If the possessor of the body part or object is already clear from the context, the definite article is used. English requires a possessive adjective (my, your, his, etc.).

  • Él se lava las manos. / He washes his hands.
  • Ponte el abrigo. / Put on your coat.
  • Me duele la cabeza. / My head hurts.

Titles

Use the article when you are speaking about someone using their title, but not when speaking directly to that person.

  • El doctor García no está. / Doctor García is not here.
  • La profesora Smith llegó tarde. / Professor Smith arrived late.
  • Buenos días, doctor García. / Good morning, Doctor García. (speaking to the person: no article)

Exception: the titles don and doña never take an article: don Juan.

Languages, subjects and disciplines

Languages and academic disciplines require an article when they function as the subject.

  • El español es un idioma hermoso. / Spanish is a beautiful language.
  • Me gusta la historia. / I like history.

However, they do not take an article after verbs like hablar, estudiar, aprender:

  • Hablo español.
  • Estudio historia.

Percentages, prices and measurements

  • El 20% de los estudiantes aprobó. / 20% of the students passed.
  • Cuesta cinco euros el kilo. / It costs five euros a kilo.
  • El parking cuesta un euro la hora. / Parking costs one euro per hour.
