quiero, puedo, necesito, tengo que

quiero, necesito, puedo

These verbs work like in English: the second verb stays in its -r form.

  • quiero = I want
  • necesito = I need
  • puedo = I can (think power)
  • intento = I try (think attempt)

Examples:

  • Quiero comer paella. = I want to eat paella.
  • Necesito ir al banco. = I need to go to the bank.
  • Hoy no puedo ir al banco. = Today I can't go to the bank.
  • Intento ir a la cama pronto. = I try to go to bed early.

Typical mistakes:

  • Intento a ir a la cama. → incorrect
  • Intento ir a la cama. → correct

tener que

tener (to have) + que = to have to.

que is the word that changes the meaning.

  • tengo que = I have to
  • tienes que = you have to
  • tiene que = he/she has to

Examples:

  • Tengo que trabajar. = I have to work.
  • ¿Qué tienes que hacer mañana? = What do you have to do tomorrow?
  • Mi amigo tiene que descansar. = My friend has to rest.
  • No puedo venir el sábado, tengo que trabajar. = I can't come on Saturday, I have to work.

Typical mistakes:

  • Tengo trabajar mañana. → incorrect
  • Tengo que trabajar mañana. → correct

debo

debo = I must (think debit: something you owe).

It sounds more serious than tengo que. In daily life, tengo que is more common.

  • Debo ir. = I must go.

Two of them together

Only the first verb changes. The others stay in the -r form.

Examples:

  • Voy a intentar desayunar. = I'm going to try to have breakfast.
  • Quiero poder hablar español. = I want to be able to speak Spanish.
  • Voy a tener que trabajar. = I'm going to have to work.
