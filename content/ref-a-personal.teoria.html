veo a mi amigo

a before a person

When a person receives the action of the verb, Spanish puts a (to) in front of that person.

English has nothing in that place: I see my friend.

It goes with names and with people like mi amigo (my friend) or mi madre (my mother).

  • ver a = to see (someone)
  • visitar a = to visit (someone)
  • llamar a = to call (someone)
  • ayudar a = to help (someone)
  • esperar a = to wait for (someone)

Examples:

  • Veo a María. = I see María.
  • Quiero visitar a mi madre. = I want to visit my mother.
  • Voy a llamar a Pedro mañana. = I'm going to call Pedro tomorrow.
  • ¿Puedes ayudar a mi hermano? = Can you help my brother?

Typical mistakes:

  • Llamo mi madre. → incorrect
  • Llamo a mi madre. → correct

Only for people

With things, there is no a.

Examples:

  • Veo a mi amigo. = I see my friend.
  • Veo el coche. = I see the car.

Why the a

In Spanish the person who does the action can also go after the verb: ¿Viene María? = Is María coming?

So the a shows who receives the action.

  • Llama María. = María is calling.
  • Llama a María. = She is calling María.
