veo a mi amigo

a before a person

When a person receives the action of the verb, Spanish puts a (to) in front of that person.

English has nothing in that place: I see my friend.

It goes with names and with people like mi amigo (my friend) or mi madre (my mother).

Examples:

  • Veo a María. = I see María.
  • Quiero visitar a mi madre. = I want to visit my mother.

Typical mistake:

  • Llamo mi madre. → incorrect
  • Llamo a mi madre. → correct

Not with things

With things, there is no a.

Examples:

  • Veo a mi amigo. = I see my friend.
  • Veo el coche. = I see the car.

Why the a

In English it is easy to tell who does the action and who receives it, by the position of the names in the sentence:

  • María calls Manuel.

In Spanish, however, the person who does the action can go before or after the verb.
These two sentences mean the same:

  • María llama a Manuel. = María calls Manuel.
  • Llama María a Manuel. = María calls Manuel.

So it is not possible to know who does the action and who receives it only by the position of the names.
That is why the a is needed: it shows clearly who receives the action.

  • Llama María. = María is calling.
  • Llama a María. = He/She is calling María.
